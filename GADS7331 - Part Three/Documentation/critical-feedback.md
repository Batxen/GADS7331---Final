Critical Engagement With Feedback:

Within this segment of the final submission I will be looking at the feedback I have received from the 24-bit Games game developer conference. 

Going into the event I was expecting to experience two major opinions regarding how my project was going to be received: those who did not want to participate within a video game project related to AI and those who could look past it. I was worried that going into the event a great many would not want to assist in a project related to AI.   
A major worry I carried going in was the idea that people would see my concept for LLM integration to be incredibly niche or have limited use cases. 

In regards to the project itself the two aspects of the project I expected to be spoken about the most was the grey box prototype state it was in prior to the conference and the LLM itself.   
The reason why I expected the grey box prototype state of the project to receive a lot of feedback is due to grey box prototyping being used primarily during the early stages of development where visuals are not a priority but rather the assurance of gameplay mechanics is. It can come off as ugly or unfinished and as such I believed that it was going to catch a lot of criticism during first impressions.   
The LLM itself I believed to be another section of the project to receive the most amount of feedback. It was the main focus of the project and as such it was what was going to be discussed most during these interviews. I was going to be introducing my project by explaining what it was that I was doing and the application of the LLM itself and where it can be used effectively. 

My expectations in comparison to reality were in a positive way aligned in some ways but also unaligned in others. My expectations around the reaction to the use of AI, the visuals, and the concept were not the reality of what I experienced. While I was correct in assuming that these three focal points would be the talking points I also experienced a lot of support from the interviewees who gave good advice both in regards to the project itself but also to resources I could use to improve myself as a game developer.   
There were individuals who did not want to take part in the feedback session and declined due to personal values or believing they could not give meaningful feedback to assist in my project. 

I was surprised when I received feedback centred around my participation in events such as Procjam (A game jam centred around procedural generation) and using text-to-speech to further improve the project. I was being given tools outside of the module project to improve my skills and it was not something I expected to be recommended.

I chose not to implement certain feedback related to the use of Trancy, text-to-speech implementation, and the BARK system. While Trancy and text-to-speech could have been good quality-of-life improvements to the project it would have required me to learn a potentially difficult and time consuming endeavour. With BARK I just could find anything about it so I am unable to consider implementing it.  

Ultimately feedback regarding the visuals and quality of dialogue are to be my focus. They are elements within my toolset and as such have the highest chance of success.

I found this experience to be uplifting. People are more supportive than you initially think.
